# Website review, 15 November 2026: will homeowners hire us from this site?

The marketing site at isdesignandbuild.com (commit 3b2f2ce) was reviewed in depth and adversarially, as a homeowner comparing contractors would see it. This note says what worked, what didn't, what was changed in this release, and what still needs the owner. The three full reports are in [docs/reviews/2026-11-15/](reviews/2026-11-15/).

## The short answer

**Before this release: impressive at first glance, not convincing enough to hire.** On a laptop the first ten seconds look designed, not templated. The doubts start straight after:

- The kitchen in the home-page hero was a stock render labelled "Design reference".
- The firm's own rooms were shown darkened, covered in traced lines, and repeated from page to page.
- The firm's best proof was hidden. It has 4.9 stars from 39 Google reviews, and Eddie Migues is named in 21 of them. It also has a BBB listing with DC, Maryland and Virginia licences, and a DC lead-paint listing. A careful buyer found all of this on Google, not on the site.
- On phones, the menu could not be scrolled, and the kitchen page jumped as it scrolled.

| Reviewer (one after another, separate lenses) | Before this release |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Desire and clarity: what a homeowner sees, reads and feels | Impressed 5/10. Would shortlist and call 4/10. |
| 2. Evidence and positioning: why believe us, why choose us over other DC firms | Believable 4/10 on the site alone, 7/10 after one Google search. Chosen over competitors 3/10. |
| 3. Execution and reach: works on every device, gets found | Works well everywhere 6/10. Found by DC buyers 6/10. |

**After this release:** the site now shows the firm's own work everywhere. Its reviews sit beside every ask, and the phone experience works. Reviewer 2 estimated 6–7/10 against competitors once the site shows reviews, licences, people and project context. This release adds the reviews and the project context. Licences, people and prices need the owner (see "Still needed from the owner").

## What worked (kept)

- **The look.** Geist type, black, white and one orange, fine rules, generous space. The drawn-then-built idea says "we design, then we build" without words.
- **The home floor-plan scene.** Bubbles become walls, a scope stamp lands and the finished kitchen opens. It is the best moment on the site.
- **Phone basics.** The headline and main button fit on the first screen, plus the fixed Call / Start a project bar, photo tiles for services and a full-screen menu.
- **Calls to action carry the service into the form.** For example, "Tell us about your kitchen" preselects the kitchen.
- **A short form.** Five questions, with timing and budget optional.
- **Nothing is faked.** No invented testimonials, awards or prices. Every gap can be filled truthfully.
- **The scope-first promise.** "We agree the scope with you before anything is built, so the estimate matches the work." Several reviews confirm it.
- **Technical foundations.** No broken links or console errors, and no sideways scrolling from 320 to 1440 px. Good reduced-motion and no-JavaScript fallbacks, valid structured data, and 319 of the old site's 399 URLs redirected.

## What didn't work

1. **The work on show wasn't ours, or wasn't enough.**
   - The hero was a stock render.
   - About 12 company photos were repeated across pages.
   - No people were shown anywhere.
   - About opened on a stock photo of a stranger in another company's branded shirt.
   - Page-top photos were darkened and scribbled over by auto-traced lines.
2. **The proof was hidden.** No rating, no review count, only two short excerpts, and no licence, insurance, warranty or certification.
3. **The words didn't win the job.**
   - Slogans filled the key lines: "Built around you", "Experience you can build on", "See the details come together".
   - FAQ answers hedged.
   - The roofing page wasn't written for someone with water coming in.
4. **Too long, and every page the same.** Every page used the same six-part template. Long pinned scroll scenes, and the drafting intro replayed on every page.
5. **The last step left questions.** Nothing said what happens after you get in touch. The phone menu had no "Start a project".
6. **Small credibility leaks.**
   - The kitchen page linked a discount retailer's stock-cabinet catalogue.
   - "Founded as I.S. Enterprises" doesn't match a 1998 incorporation.
   - "The names clients mention again and again" overstated Italo (Eddie is named in 21 reviews, Italo in 4).
7. **Technical.**
   - The phone menu couldn't scroll, so four links were unreachable.
   - Layout shift on the kitchen page on phones was 0.33 (poor).
   - Nothing measured calls or inquiries.
   - The privacy text didn't match the analytics settings.
   - Security headers had been lost.
   - 68 old URLs returned 404.
   - Three page titles had no location.
   - Galleries and the video had keyboard and accessibility gaps.

## What changed in this release

**The work and the proof**
- The home hero is now our own kitchen, with a line drawing traced by hand from that photograph. "Design reference" is gone from the first screen.
- 12 more company photographs from the old isenterprises.net site join the library, doubling it to 24.
- Every page top is now a company photograph, except lead paint. That page keeps a stock street of older rowhouses, labelled "Stock image". The 17 unused stock photos were removed.
- Page tops are lighter: a dark scrim sits only where the words are. The traced lines now fade out once the photo is built.
- Google reviews are now on the site:
  - "4.9 on Google from 39 reviews" in the home proof strip, in the facts and beside the reviews.
  - Excerpts quoted word for word, with name, job and date, chosen for each page: roofing reviews on roofing, lead-paint reviews on lead paint, and so on.
  - A new /reviews/ page.
  - Each excerpt was checked against the Google profile on 15 November 2026.
- About:
  - New headline: "Building in the DC area since 1989."
  - New lede: founded by Italo Rodriguez and Eddie Migues, formerly I.S. Enterprises.
  - The years line no longer says "founded as I.S. Enterprises".
  - Reviews at the centre.
  - An exact line about Eddie's place in the reviews.
- The portfolio now shows all 24 photos. The scarcity counters are gone, and four reviews describe older-house projects in the clients' own words.

**The words**
- Home:
  - The first screen now carries "Washington, DC area · Since 1989" and "We handle the design, the permits and the build", plus "Roof leak? Call".
  - The statement is now "One team designs it, prices it and builds it", with the scope-first promise.
  - Clearer process steps.
- New headlines and ledes on remodeling, kitchen, bathroom, whole-home, roofing and About.
- FAQ answers rewritten so they answer the question, linking to financing and lead paint where it helps.
- Roofing:
  - Call is the primary action in the page top, the closing section and the phone bar.
  - "We repair the stained ceiling and walls along with the roof."
  - An honest "water coming in now" answer.
  - The materials question is removed until the owner answers it.
- Lead paint: "We plan containment, dust control and clean-up into the scope from the first visit." The stock dust photo is gone.
- Every page ends with its own closing question.
- US spelling: "inquiry", not "enquiry".
- The wholesale cabinet catalogue is no longer linked. Its file stays at the old address, marked noindex.
- Every service page is under 200 words above its questions. The home page is at 318 words (limit 350).

**The path to inquiry**
- Contact:
  - A "What happens next" list above the form.
  - The rating and a review beside the form.
  - Warmer roofing help text.
  - The button now reads "Send my project details".
- The closing section has a filled "Start a project" button and the phone number. The header button is filled orange.
- The phone menu now has the Call / Start a project bar at its foot.
- A new Planning menu holds process, financing, lead paint, FAQs and guides. Reviews are under About.
- Guides now ask halfway through.

**Motion and length**
- The long drafting intro plays once per visit. Later pages open straight onto the photograph.
- Pinned scenes are shorter: the home floor plan runs over 240% of the screen height instead of 420%, and the drawing scenes about 40% less. On phones the drawings show finished, with every point open.
- The home statement inks in once instead of following the scroll. Years no longer count up.
- The see-through header gets a dark backing as soon as the page moves.

**Technical**
- Phone menu:
  - The whole sheet scrolls, with Lenis told to leave it alone.
  - It closes on a link tap, on Back and when the window widens.
  - The page behind it is inert.
  - Desktop dropdowns close when keyboard focus leaves.
- Layout shift on phones is now 0.000 on kitchen, whole-home and roofing (kitchen was 0.33) and 0.001 on home.
- Analytics:
  - Google's standard Tag Manager start, loaded after the page has finished loading.
  - Events for `click_to_call`, `email_click`, `start_project_click`, `generate_lead` and `inquiry_fallback_shown`.
- The privacy policy now discloses the Google tag's automatic detection of contact details, and the form note no longer says "only". Both legal pages are dated. The terms no longer describe the old email-only form.
- Safari:
  - The line drawings no longer combine dash animation with non-scaling strokes, which Safari can draw as dotted lines.
  - The plan's ellipses are now paths.
  - The header blur gets its `-webkit-` prefix.
- Accessibility:
  - Swipe galleries are focusable regions.
  - Home gallery links show a focus ring, and focus brings the photo into view.
  - The photo viewer swipes reliably.
  - The video has a pause button.
  - The "What we build" label is visible, with descriptions shown without motion.
  - Labels are 12px.
  - The phone bar sits in a landmark.
  - Larger tap targets on tablets.
  - The first screen fits on landscape phones.
- Search:
  - Locations added to the bathroom, whole-home and roofing titles.
  - Brand added to Service areas.
  - Guide titles no longer cut off.
  - `og:type` is "article" on guides.
  - The 404 page is noindex with no canonical.
  - A new share image made from our own kitchen.
  - `favicon.ico` added.
- `vercel.json`:
  - Security headers restored: nosniff, referrer and permissions policies, frame protection.
  - The two old articles and the /category/ and /author/ archives now redirect.
  - Media is cached.
- Heavy photos load smaller sizes at a lower quality. The lead-paint stock photo is cropped closer to how it is shown.

## Still needed from the owner, in priority order

1. **Licences and insurance.** The BBB lists DC licences 70103548 and 69011757, Maryland MHIC 98433 and Virginia DPOR 2705128578.
   - Confirm which are current and which DC licence is the contractor licence.
   - Confirm insurance (general liability, workers' comp).
   - Maryland requires the MHIC number on contractor websites.
   - Once confirmed, set `credentials` in `src/data/site.ts`. It then shows in the footer and on the contact page.
2. **Reviews.**
   - Approve showing the rating and these excerpts. Asking the quoted clients is courteous.
   - One five-star review (April 2020) appears to be from a member of staff. Google's rules don't allow that, so have it removed (the rating stays 4.9).
   - Reply to reviews, including the 2019 one-star.
3. **Lead-paint certification.** The DC DOEE firm listing last seen expired on 28 September 2023 (DC18-8929).
   - Send the current certificate and expiry, and the EPA RRP firm certificate.
   - Say whether clearance testing is independent.
4. **Warranty.** A public reply on Google mentions a 30-year roof material warranty and a 5-year workmanship warranty. Confirm the written warranty for remodeling and roofing. Angi says there is none; fix Angi.
5. **People.** Portraits of Italo and Eddie, their roles, three to five sentences each, who the client deals with day to day, and what "I.S" stands for.
6. **Projects.**
   - Before and after photos of older DC homes, with permission.
   - What 643 Girard St NE and 1326 Montello Ave NE were: new builds or conversions.
   - A licence for the listing photographs. Eight carry a photographer's copyright, "Derek and Vee".
7. **Money and time.**
   - Typical price ranges for each kind of project and the minimum job.
   - Typical durations.
   - Whether the first visit is free.
   - The deposit and payment schedule.
   - How changes are priced.
   - How fast inquiries get a reply.
8. **Identity.**
   - Google shows "Suite 300 #12" and closes at 4 on Mondays; the site shows "Suite 300" and 9 to 5. Pick one and make it identical everywhere.
   - Update BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, MapQuest, Andersen and DOEE to the new name and phone. BBB still shows (202) 388-6799 and 1324 H St NE.
9. **Roofing.** The roof types and materials you install, the emergency and out-of-hours policy, and roof photos.
10. **Services to sell or not.** Windows (Andersen Certified Contractor?), porches and masonry, accessibility work, owners who live abroad (three reviews), and commercial or government work.
11. **Analytics.**
    - In Tag Manager, add GA4 event tags for the new events and mark `generate_lead` and `click_to_call` as key events.
    - Decide whether to keep the automatic detection of user-provided data. If it is switched off, the privacy paragraph about it can go.
    - Have counsel review the privacy and terms changes.
12. **The headline.** "Your home. In good hands." stays because you approved it. Reviewer 1 points out that it echoes Allstate's "You're in good hands". An alternative that matches the drawn-then-built opening: "Designed with you. Built by us."
13. **The cabinet catalogue PDF.** Keep it (unlinked, noindex) or delete it.
14. **The inquiry form.** It is still to be wired up (Resend key in Vercel), as planned.
