# Website review, 19 November 2026: will homeowners like it, be impressed and hire us?

The live site at isdesignandbuild.com (commit 54085f6) was reviewed in depth and adversarially by three reviewers, run one after another, each with its own lens and no overlap: **the eye** (what a homeowner sees and feels), **the buyer** (what they read and believe, and whether they would hire us over BOWA, Case, Landis and others) and **the inspector** (does it work on every device, accessibly and fast, and does it get found). The full reports are in [docs/reviews/2026-11-19/](reviews/2026-11-19/). This note says what worked, what didn't, what was changed in this release and what still needs the owner. The inquiry form's delivery was out of scope, as asked.

## The short answer

**Before this release: a homeowner would admire the website, not the work, and would believe us without choosing us.** The site looked like an expensive website rather than an expensive builder. It showed our own rooms at about half their real brightness, covered them with words on a phone, and made the visitor scroll through screens of line drawings before reaching them. The words were honest and modest but said nothing only we could say. The facts a $150k buyer checks first (licence, insurance, warranty, who will be in my house, rough cost, timing) were missing, and the site's own chatbot admitted it. Technically it was sound, but the Intercom chat bubble sat on top of the phone's main buttons, including "Call" on the roofing page.

By persona (would get in touch): Capitol Hill rowhouse kitchen and baths 6, Chevy Chase addition 4, roof leak at night 7 (despite the pictures), landlord with lead paint 4.

**After this release:** every page opens on a bright photograph of the service it sells, the work comes first, the copy answers the buyer's main objections where they arise, and nothing covers the phone's buttons. What still holds the scores down is proof only the owner can supply: licences, insurance, warranty, people, prices and more projects (see "Still needed from the owner").

## What worked (kept)

- **The photographs themselves.** All 24 company photos are real, sharp and bright. Shown plainly, the kitchens and bathrooms stand up next to the competitors.
- **One consistent, custom look.** The type, black sheets, fine rules and single orange are crafted. Nothing looks like a template.
- **The drawn-then-built idea.** No DC competitor does it, and it says "we design, then we build" without words. The owner likes it, and it stays.
- **Honesty.** No invented claims anywhere. Reviews are verbatim, with name, job and month. The labels "Our project" and "Stock image" are truthful.
- **The 4.9 Google rating from 39 reviews** beside every ask, and reviews that answer objections in clients' own words ("We did receive cheaper quotes … but we chose Eddie").
- **Roofing leads with the phone**, and the form asks whether water is coming in.
- **Technical foundations.** No sideways scrolling at any of 8 sizes, a working menu, viewer, galleries and FAQs, good no-JavaScript and reduced-motion versions, fast first screens (LCP 0.8 to 1.3 s on a throttled phone, no layout shift), clean structured data and redirects.

## What didn't work

1. **The work was hidden.** Page-top photos showed at 42 to 55% of their real brightness. On phones, words covered the room, leaving a strip of ceiling. The home page took 5.2 s to show its kitchen, then left a wireframe over it. Real kitchens started on screen 4 to 5 of service pages, behind pinned drawings. The home page played unlabelled stock footage.
2. **Service pages didn't show the service.** Additions opened on the space under a deck, whole-home on a bedroom, roofing on a rooftop lounge. The portfolio was a grid of small, same-size thumbnails, and the same few photos repeated from page to page.
3. **Nothing only we could say.** "Your home. In good hands." echoes Allstate. "In-house" was repeated ten times without proof. Our real differentiators sat inside review excerpts: the founders since 1989, Eddie on site, and one firm for the kitchen, the roof, the leak-damaged ceiling and the lead paint.
4. **The buyer's objections went unanswered.** There was nothing on licences, insurance, permits, designers or commercial work. The roofing page promised "Call us today" but leads to voicemail after hours, and "Send photos" opened a form that can't take photos. The lead page was written for renovators, not for the landlord who needs the work done, and lead paint was missing from the menu, the form and the services. The phone number wasn't in the desktop header.
5. **The chat hurt more than it helped.** On every phone the Intercom bubble covered the bottom bar's main button, and on the contact page it covered "Send my project details". It loaded about 1 MB on every page view and froze a mid-range phone for 0.5 to 1 s around 14 s after landing. It also greets visitors as "IS AI AI Agent".
6. **Search and measurement.** Six titles had no DC term, and most H1s named neither the service nor the place. /about, /contact and /blog returned 404, and the sitemap had no dates. **No lead click reaches Google Analytics:** the site sends `click_to_call`, `email_click` and `start_project_click`, but the Tag Manager container has no triggers for them.
7. **Contrast.** Text over light photos (the header, small labels) measured 1.9 to 3.0:1 in places, where 4.5:1 is needed.

## What changed in this release

**Show the work, first and bright**
- Page tops now shade only behind the words, which sit in a left column on desktop. The room shows at 94 to 98% of its real brightness, up from about 85%, and the whole frame at 57 to 72%, up from 42 to 55%. Every headline, lede, header link and label was measured against the actual pixels behind it, and all meet WCAG AA.
- Phones and upright tablets put the room at the top of the screen at full brightness, with the words on black below it. The headline and main button are on the first screen of every page.
- The home opening keeps the owner's drawn-then-built idea but finishes in about 2.6 s instead of 5.2 s, and the drawing then fades away. **To restore the old timing, set `--t-built: 3.4s` in `src/styles/hero.css`.**
- Home order: hero, then what we build, the rooms we've built, how we work, a band of our own rooftop (replacing the stock video), the facts, the reviews and the ask. On phones the process plan is shown finished, with every step, instead of pinned. The pinned plan is shorter on desktop.
- Each service page opens on that service: whole-home on the kitchen open to the stair and living room, additions on the rear of a whole house, bathroom on the bright marble bath. Galleries now come straight after the page top on kitchen, bathroom and whole-home, and additions has a new gallery of decks, terraces and rooftops. Main pages give each photograph one role.
- The portfolio leads with one photograph at full width, then groups rooms (kitchens; bathrooms and powder rooms; decks, terraces and rooftops; bedrooms) in two large columns, and it opens straight onto the work.
- On phones, bands show the whole photograph above their words, galleries show rooms at 4:3 rather than tall slivers, and page endings are shorter: no repeated contact grid and no giant wordmark.
- The reviews page has strips of project photographs between its groups, and the About page's empty gap is tightened.
- The "Page / Photo" title block over every photo is now one small "Our project" or "Stock image" label.

**Say what only we can say, and answer the objections**
- The home lede names the founders and the range: "Kitchens, bathrooms, additions and whole homes, designed, permitted and built by one firm, plus roofs and lead-safe work. Founded by Italo Rodriguez and Eddie Migues."
- The home facts now read "4.9 / 1989 / Eddie: our co-founder, named again and again in our Google reviews". The proof line names the founders instead of "One in-house team". The home reviews lead with Frank Jackson: "Eddie was there pretty much every day".
- Roofing:
  - Says what happens out of hours: leave your address and number and we'll call you back.
  - "Email photos of your roof" opens an email.
  - Adds "Until we get there" advice.
  - Closes with "Water coming in? Call (202) 519-1831."
- Lead paint:
  - Now "Lead-safe work for older DC homes and rentals".
  - Adds a landlord section ("Renting it out? Tell us if you have a DOEE notice or order …") and the Shedd review.
  - Lead paint is now a service in the menu, the home page, the form, the closing shortcuts and the footer.
- New FAQs: licences and insurance, permits, living at home during the work, lead paint, working with your architect or designer, and commercial and government work. Laurion's "the pricing was fair" review now sits beside them.
- Reviews now sit beside the objection they answer. Bathroom adds the accessibility review, additions the permits review, and whole-home the owners-abroad and tight-schedule reviews. The contact page now quotes Peyton's "didn't … try to sell me a dream".
- Clearer headlines and ledes:
  - Additions: "More room, with the permits handled."
  - Service areas: "DC, Montgomery County and Northern Virginia."
  - FAQs: "Straight answers before you start."
  - Kitchen, bathroom and whole-home ledes now lead with the promise; the area follows.
- Navigation:
  - Services ▾ (with lead paint), Roofing, Our work, Reviews and About ▾.
  - The phone number appears in the desktop header.
  - The process page uses the home page's step names.
- Contact:
  - Budget is now a choice of ranges (optional), and lead paint is a project type.
  - A note explains that the email address still carries the former name.
- Privacy: the policy now discloses the Intercom chat (dated 19 November 2026).

**Works for everyone, and gets found**
- Chat:
  - Intercom's own bubble is hidden, and the ~1 MB Messenger loads only when a visitor taps "Chat with us". Before that it costs nothing.
  - Returning visitors with an open conversation get it back automatically.
  - On phones, chat is an icon inside the Call / Start a project bar, so nothing floats over the main buttons. There is no chat button on the contact page.
  - Opening the chat pushes a `chat_open` event.
- The phone bar appears after 160 px of scrolling instead of 480 px.
- Contrast fixes:
  - A firmer shade under the header on photographs, and nav links at full strength.
  - A lighter orange on near-opaque backing for photo labels.
  - White band labels.
  - Process step numbers no longer fade with their step.
- Search:
  - Every service page's H1 starts with a small service-and-place line, for example "Kitchen remodeling in Washington, DC".
  - DC terms added to the titles of About, Reviews, Our work, Contact, Process and FAQs.
  - Shorter About description.
  - Schema adds lead-safe renovation and a logo.
  - The sitemap now carries last-modified dates.
  - /about, /contact and /blog now redirect.
- The skip link's target takes focus. Photo traces were added for the three new page-top photographs.
- Checked:
  - `npm run check`: 0 errors.
  - `npm run build`: 24 pages.
  - Every page at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px and on a landscape phone: no console errors, no sideways scroll, one H1 each, alt text on every image.
  - Reduced-motion and no-JavaScript versions complete.
  - In Chromium only. Safari and Firefox still need a real-device pass.
- Live on 19 November 2026 at about 11:18 UTC (commit 60b87aa).
  - All 19 public pages return 200 on the new build, with no page errors and no `noindex`.
  - The chat makes no request to Intercom until it is tapped, then opens the Messenger on phone and desktop.
  - The new redirects, the sitemap dates and the security headers are live.
  - Reviewer 3's "the desktop chat launcher never appeared" was its headless test browser: Intercom does not start for one. In a normal browser it opens on desktop.

## Still needed from the owner, in priority order

1. **Licences, insurance, warranty.** This is the biggest gap against BOWA, Case and Landis.
   - Confirm the DC licence, MHIC number and Virginia licence (the BBB lists DC 70103548 and 69011757, MHIC 98433 and VA 2705128578), the insurance and the written warranty or after-care policy.
   - Then set `credentials` in `src/data/site.ts`, and replace the interim FAQ answer "Ask us for our licence numbers and certificate of insurance when we first talk".
   - Maryland requires the MHIC number on contractor advertising.
2. **Lead-paint certification.** Confirm the current DOEE abatement certification and the EPA RRP firm number. The page now says "lead-safe work" and avoids claiming "abatement" until then. Once confirmed, the H1 and title can say "Lead-paint abatement", which is what landlords search for.
3. **Google Tag Manager (P0).** The setup is now in code: [docs/tracking.md](tracking.md).
   - Create a Google Cloud service account, give it Publish on GTM-WFP56VXL and Editor on the GA4 property, and store its JSON key as `GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON` (steps in that note). An API key does not work.
   - Then `npm run tracking:publish` builds the tags, marks the key events and publishes, and the switch from GTM-NSZWBWT2 to GTM-WFP56VXL can go live.
   - Until then, no lead is measured.
4. **Intercom settings (no code).**
   - Rename the agent from "IS AI AI Agent" to "I.S Design + Build".
   - Have the greeting give the phone number for leaks.
   - Ask for a phone number and address on leak questions.
   - Turn off session recording and proactive messages if unused.
   - Close the one test conversation reviewer 2 sent from /roofing/ on 19 November at about 10:37 UTC.
5. **After-hours roofing.** Does anyone return calls after hours, and when? One line ("we call back first thing next business day", or a 24-hour number) wins or loses the night-time leak caller.
6. **People and projects.**
   - Photos and one line each for Italo and Eddie.
   - Who designs, and with what credentials.
   - Photographs of a real addition, a roof job, lead-safe work and older DC rowhouses, with neighbourhoods.
   - Which photos belong to which project, so the portfolio can show projects rather than rooms.
7. **Money and time.** Typical price ranges and durations, whether the first visit is free, and whether the agreed price is fixed. A free visit or a fixed price would be the strongest line against Landis, which publishes its prices.
8. **The headline.** "Your home. In good hands." stays because the owner approved it. Two reviewers in a row recommend replacing it (it echoes Allstate). Option: "Kitchens to roofs. One DC firm since 1989."
9. **"In-house".** Interior copy still says "one in-house team" and "our own team" (owner-approved copy). Confirm which trades really are in-house, or soften it.
10. **Email and name.** Consider an @isdesignandbuild.com address on the same inbox, because a mismatched email domain worries buyers. Use one brand spelling on Google, Intercom and the site.
11. **Northern Virginia.** Name the towns served, or drop it from the ledes.
12. **Real devices.** Check iPhone Safari and Firefox (menu, viewer, swipe galleries, chat).
