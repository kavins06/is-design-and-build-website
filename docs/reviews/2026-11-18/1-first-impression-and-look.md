# Review 1 of 3: first impression and look

Date: 2026-11-18. Reviewer 1 (the eye): what a DC homeowner sees and feels before reading closely.

Method: the built site at http://127.0.0.1:4321/ (same commit as production). All 23 sitemap pages walked as a first-time visitor (fresh browser per page) at 1440×900 and on an emulated iPhone 13, one screen at a time with real input (mouse wheel on desktop, touch swipes on the phone). Six pages also at 1366×768, four on an iPad Pro 11, home and kitchen with reduced motion. Openings filmed every 0.2 s for 7 s. Every photo checked at 100%. Seven DC-area competitors captured. Proposed fixes for the photo shading and the phone ending were tried in the browser (no repo changes) and measured. Evidence tags such as [E3] point to section 6.

## 1. Verdict

The site looks like an expensive website, not like an expensive builder. The type, the dark drawing-sheet system and the motion are polished and consistent, but they bury the work: every page-top photograph is shown at about half its real brightness (less than half on phones), the galleries start four to seven screens down, and the work itself (about 20 listing-style photos of two or three new-build-style NE DC houses, with no people, no older homes, no additions and no roof jobs) cannot stand next to BOWA, Case or Anthony Wilder. A DC homeowner will admire the website, not the work, and will rarely picture this firm in their own house.

## 2. What works: keep it

1. **One consistent, custom-built look.** Every page opens the same way: photograph, large white display type, one orange action, a small drawing-sheet title block [E1]. Nothing looks like a contractor template. Nothing was broken on any of the 23 pages at four sizes.
2. **The photography is technically professional.** All 24 company photos are sharp at 100%, level and evenly exposed [E9]. The problems are which photos are shown and how, not focus or exposure.
3. **Shown plainly, the photos look good.** The desktop portfolio grid and the full-screen photo viewer are the best screens on the site [E22 portfolio/01, E15]. The phone service tiles on the home page work well [E12 phone-home screens 05–06].
4. **The drawn-then-built idea is distinctive (owner decision).** The hand-traced lines land exactly on the kitchen, and the moment the room wipes in, at about 4.6 s, is the best moment on the site [E6, left frame]. Keep the idea; make it faster and let the lines go (P1-1).
5. **The service-page drawings are crisp and specific:** a kitchen work triangle, bathroom services behind the walls, a pre-1978 rowhouse facade for lead paint [E22 kitchen-remodeling/02]. Keep them, after the photos.
6. **The phone patterns are designed, not squeezed:** a full-screen black menu with large type, the fixed Call / Start a project bar (Call becomes the primary action on roofing), swipe galleries, static drawings with every point open, and only one pinned section on phones (home, 1.8 screens) [E15, E12].
7. **Moving between pages is quick and smooth.** The second page in a visit is built about 1 s after the click, with a clean wipe [E15 nav].
8. **Reduced motion gets a complete, calm site:** photos visible at once, galleries as grids, nothing pinned [E17].
9. **The favicon** (white "IS" with an orange plus on black) reads at small sizes [E18].

### F1 [P0] The work is shown at half brightness, and on phones it disappears

- **What the homeowner experiences.** Every page opens on a grey, gloomy version of a bright white room. On a phone the room cannot be read at all: the 18 first screens are interchangeable dark smears behind white text, and you cannot tell the kitchen page from the roofing page by its picture. Every competitor captured shows a recognisable room or house on its first phone screen.
- **Evidence.** Displayed brightness is 44–56% of the original on every desktop page top (contact 31%). Even the right 40% of the frame, which a CSS comment says "stays bright", shows at 67–72%. Phones: 32–46% [E4, E23]. Side by side [E3]. All first screens [E1, E2]. Competitors [E14 bench-phone-1, bench-desktop-1]. The mid-page bands are washed the same way, and the video plays at 62% opacity.
- **Cause.** src/styles/scenes.css:18-21 (three overlaid gradients reaching 85% black); scenes.css:25-28 (phones: 94% black at the bottom, still 55% black three-quarters of the way up, clear only in the top tenth); src/styles/hero.css:91-97 (home: a 75° gradient from 85% to 55% to 12%, plus top and bottom gradients); hero.css:218-220 (phones); scenes.css:140 (bands); src/styles/home.css:468 (video at .62).

- **What the homeowner experiences.** A buyer who has just looked at BOWA, Case or Anthony Wilder has seen custom millwork, stone, period detail, house exteriors and named projects. Here they see two or three similar new-build-style rowhouse interiors: gloss-white slab cabinets, black pull-down faucets, busy mosaic backsplashes and standard stainless appliances, staged with a "home sweet home" utensil pot, COFFEE and TEA canisters and starfish art, shot ultra-wide in the style of a sales listing.

No person appears anywhere. No finished house is shown from the front. The only older home shown as a subject is a stock photo on the lead-paint page; older rowhouses appear only in the background of two deck photos. No addition. No roof job. The only video is generic footage of hands leafing through plans [E10]. Competitors [E14].
- **Cause.** The content: 24 photos in src/assets/photos, listed in src/data/photos.ts:31-56. The business notes name two portfolio projects (643 Girard St NE and 1326 Montello Ave NE).
